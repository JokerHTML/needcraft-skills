# Worked example: complete per-post SEO output

**Status: example.** The block below is a complete SEO output for an invented example post. The post was not published; the phrases were chosen from audience wording, not measured with a keyword tool, and no volumes are claimed. Copy the structure, replace every line with your own post's facts.

## The post

A carousel teaching beginners to grow tomatoes on a balcony.

## SEO output block

- **Primary search phrase:** "grow tomatoes on a balcony"
- **Reader intent:** do — the reader wants to grow them.
- **Secondary phrases (5):**
  1. "balcony tomato growing"
  2. "growing tomatoes in pots"
  3. "tomato plants for small spaces"
  4. "when to plant tomatoes"
  5. "balcony vegetable garden"
- **Platform-native title/headline:** "Grow Tomatoes on a Balcony (Even With No Garden)"
- **Caption/description (Instagram):** "No garden? You can still grow tomatoes on a balcony — this carousel shows the pot, the soil, and the watering routine, step by step. No experience needed."
- **On-screen headline:** "Tomatoes, on a balcony."
- **Opening spoken phrase (video version):** "Here's how to grow tomatoes on a balcony — even with no garden."
- **Hashtags (2):** #BalconyGarden #GrowYourOwn
- **Alt text:** "Carousel slide showing a tomato plant in a pot on a balcony railing, with a watering can beside it."
- **Verified link destination:** the growing guide the post is based on — check it on the day you publish and record the date here. No link is claimed until you have opened it.
- **Tie-break note:** "growing tomatoes in pots" lost on specificity to the primary phrase; it becomes a secondary phrase and the subject of a later post.
- **Post-publication review note:** at 24h check saves and profile visits; at 72h check Search Console queries for the primary phrase (own site only); at 7d compare against the previous tutorial post and record whether the primary phrase drove any search traffic. No ranking is promised.

## Why this block passes the skill's rules

- One primary phrase, with intent named.
- Five secondaries, all genuinely relevant to the post.
- No volume, difficulty, or ranking figure anywhere — the phrases are labelled as chosen from audience wording.
- The keyword sits in the first line of the caption, in the on-screen headline, and in the opening spoken phrase.
- The link destination is checked on the day of publishing and dated.
- The tie-break loser is recorded with its reason, so the 7-day review has a ready-made next test.
